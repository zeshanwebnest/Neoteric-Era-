01 — The point

Paid search is the only channel that starts immediately.

That makes it valuable for two distinct reasons: it produces enquiries while slower channels build, and it tells you within a week which message converts — evidence your SEO and content work can then borrow.

It also makes it the easiest place to waste money quietly. A campaign can look busy and productive while most of the budget goes to queries with no purchase intent, counted against a conversion event that fires on a page view.

On our fee structure Management is a flat monthly fee. Your media budget is paid directly to Google from your own account, and we take no percentage of it. A spend-based fee rewards an agency for spending more of your money — we would rather not have that conflict in the room during a budget conversation.

02 — Symptoms

What a struggling account looks like.

We see the same handful of faults in most accounts we inherit. None of them are exotic; all of them are expensive.

Symptom 01

Rising cost per lead

Spend is flat and enquiries are falling. Usually match types have loosened and broad queries are absorbing the budget of specific ones.

Symptom 02

Leads that never qualify

Volume looks acceptable and sales says none of it is real. The conversion event is measuring the wrong action, so optimisation is chasing the wrong signal.

Symptom 03

Performance Max as a black box

Budget goes in and nobody can explain what it bought. Asset groups and audience signals were never structured, so automation had nothing to learn from.

Symptom 04

Shopping campaigns underperforming

Almost always a feed problem: missing attributes, weak titles, poor category mapping. Bid changes cannot fix bad product data.

Symptom 05

Generic landing pages

High-intent clicks arriving on the homepage. The visitor has to find what the ad promised, and most of them will not bother.

Symptom 06

Reporting nobody can act on

A monthly deck of impressions and click-through rates with no line connecting spend to revenue or to the enquiries that closed.

03 — Capabilities

Campaign types, and when each earns its place.

Most accounts should run two or three of these well rather than all six thinly.

Search Ads

The core of most accounts. Tight query control, disciplined negatives, and ad copy matched to the specific intent of each group.

Shopping Ads

Feed quality before bidding. Titles, attributes, category mapping and stock accuracy determine most of the outcome.

Performance Max

Effective when asset groups, audience signals and exclusions are deliberately structured. Wasteful when it is used as a default.

Display Ads

Best used for retargeting with frequency caps and placement exclusions rather than broad prospecting.

YouTube Ads

Worthwhile where the offer genuinely benefits from demonstration, and where you can support a longer attribution window.

PPC Management

The ongoing discipline: query mining, negatives, bid strategy, creative testing and honest budget recommendations.

04 — Process

First ninety days.

Month one is about accuracy. Efficiency comes once the data is trustworthy.

Week 1

Audit and tracking

Before changing bids we verify what is being counted as a conversion. Roughly a third of the accounts we review are optimising toward an event that does not represent a real enquiry.

Weeks 2–3

Waste elimination and restructure

Search term mining, a proper negative framework, match-type tightening, and campaign structure rebuilt so specific terms are not outbid by broad ones.

Weeks 3–6

Creative and landing alignment

Ad copy rewritten per intent group, extensions completed, and dedicated landing pages built where the intent justifies one. This is where the same-team advantage shows most.

Weeks 6–12

Bid strategy and scaling

With clean data and enough conversions, smart bidding has something real to optimise against. Budget then moves toward what is demonstrably working.

Ongoing

Monthly review

Cost per qualified lead by campaign, spend by intent group, recovered waste, and a recommendation on whether budget should increase, hold or reduce. Sometimes the answer is reduce.
