At every tier

Included whatever you spend

These are not upsells at any level, because charging for them would mean charging for things you should never have had to ask for.

You own everything

Domain, code, designs, content, Google Ads account, Analytics, Search Console. All in your name from day one, never in a reseller account you would have to argue your way out of.

No setup fee

Onboarding, audits, access setup and the first month's planning are part of the retainer. A setup fee is a price increase with a different label on it.

A named person who does the work

Not an account manager relaying messages to a team you never meet. Our hours overlap the US business day and updates are written for asynchronous review.

Reporting that includes the misses

Led by qualified enquiries rather than sessions, and it states what did not move and what we are doing about it. A report containing only wins is a sales document.

Bar-compliant advertising

Copy is written against your state's advertising rules on claims, disclaimers and testimonials. Getting a firm cited by its own bar is not a marketing result.

An honest no

If the budget cannot reach the goal, or the timeline is not physically possible, we say so on the first call. A declined project costs us one proposal; a mismatched one costs us both months.

Questions about the money

Because withholding them wastes both sides' time. A firm with a $2,000 budget and a firm with a $20,000 budget need different answers, and finding that out on a sales call after two weeks of chasing helps nobody. The packages here cover most of what we are asked for. Where a firm needs something outside them we still quote to scope, and we say so rather than bending a package to fit.

No, and no legitimate agency should offer it. ABA Model Rule 5.4 prohibits a lawyer from sharing legal fees with a non-lawyer, and most state bars mirror it. An arrangement that pays us a cut of your fees, or a bounty per signed client, puts your licence at risk to save you a fixed monthly cost.

We charge a flat fee for defined work, which is the arrangement the rules contemplate. If another agency offers you pay-per-case, check it against your state bar before you sign it.
