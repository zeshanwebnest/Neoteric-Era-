02 — Timelines & results

How long things actually take.

The biggest variable in a project timeline is not development speed. It is how quickly content and approvals come back.

The honest answer

Typical ranges

Marketing site
4 to 8 weeks
Ecommerce
8 to 14 weeks
SEO signal
3 to 6 months
Paid search
Readable within weeks

A straightforward marketing site is typically four to eight weeks from signed brief to launch. Ecommerce and custom applications run longer.

The single biggest variable is not development speed — it is how quickly content and approvals come back from your side. Most overruns we have seen trace to a decision-maker who was not identified at the start and who appears in week five with opinions.

Technical fixes can move things within weeks. Content and authority work realistically takes three to six months to show in revenue, and longer in competitive sectors.

Anyone promising rankings in thirty days is either working on terms nobody searches, or is about to do something that will cost you later.

A monthly report led by the metric that matters to your business — usually qualified enquiries or revenue, not sessions. It states what we did, what moved, what did not move and what we are doing about it.

Tests that failed appear in the report. A report that only contains wins is a sales document.

Every build includes a post-launch period for defects, which are our responsibility and not billable. Beyond that, maintenance is optional and priced as a retainer covering updates, patching, backups and monitoring.

Plenty of clients handle it themselves, and we hand over documentation that makes that a realistic choice rather than a nominal one.

04 — Technical & platforms

What we build on, and why.

WordPress Shopify Laravel React Next.js

The platform follows the requirement. We do not have one stack we push onto every brief.

On platform choice

WordPress and Shopify for most marketing and ecommerce work, Laravel where a custom back end is genuinely required, and React or Next.js for application-style front ends.

The platform follows the requirement. An agency that builds everything on one stack is telling you about their hiring, not about your project.

Often, yes, and it is frequently the better call. A rebuild that discards existing search authority can set you back a year, and the recovery is rarely as quick as the loss.

If the foundations are sound we will say so and quote to improve rather than replace — even though the rebuild would be the larger project for us.

That is the arrangement we prefer. Run separately, the two compete: paid bids against terms you already rank for, and organic gets no benefit from the conversion data paid generates within days.
