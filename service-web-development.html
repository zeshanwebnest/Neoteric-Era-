01 — The point

What a build is actually for.

The commercial purpose of a website is to convert a higher proportion of the attention you have already paid for. Everything else — the platform, the aesthetic, the technology — is a means to that.

This shapes real decisions. It is why we set a performance budget before designing, because a two-second delay costs more conversions than most layout choices gain. It is why we insist on editable content models, because a site your team cannot update stops reflecting your business within a year. And it is why analytics and conversion tracking are built during development rather than added after launch, when the first month of data is already lost.

  • Speed as a requirement. Agreed Core Web Vitals targets, tested at every milestone.
  • Search-ready structure. Semantic markup, clean URLs and schema from the first template.
  • Accessible by default. Keyboard order, contrast and labelling verified, not assumed.
  • Editable without us. Content models your team can maintain independently.
  • Measured from day one. GA4 and conversion tracking wired in during build.
  • Built to extend. Templates designed for the pages you will need in two years.

    • 02 — Symptoms

    The problems that bring people here.

    If two or more of these describe your situation, a rebuild is usually cheaper than continuing to compensate for the current site.

    Symptom 01

    Paid traffic converts badly

    Ad spend is producing clicks and few enquiries. Usually the landing experience is slow, the page answers a different question from the ad, or the form asks for too much too early.

    Symptom 02

    Every change needs a developer

    Your marketing team raises a ticket to change a headline. The site has become a bottleneck rather than a tool, and campaigns are being shaped around what is easy to edit.

    Symptom 03

    Mobile experience is an afterthought

    Most of your traffic is on a phone and the site was designed on a desktop. Tap targets are small, the navigation is awkward, and the form is the worst part of the journey.

    Symptom 04

    Plugin sprawl has become the architecture

    Thirty-plus plugins, overlapping page builders, and nobody willing to update anything in case it breaks. The site now carries risk rather than just technical debt.

    Symptom 05

    The site no longer describes the business

    You have moved upmarket, added services or changed who you sell to, and the website still presents the company you were three years ago.

    Symptom 06

    Growth requires pages you cannot build

    You need a page per city, service or product line, and the current setup means each one is a manual, inconsistent, hand-built exception.

    04 — Process

    How a build runs, week by week.

    Indicative for a marketing site. Ecommerce and application builds extend the development and QA phases rather than adding new ones.

    Week 1

    Discovery and brief

    Commercial goals, target metrics, stakeholder map, technical audit of anything being carried over, and the content schedule that will determine whether we hit the launch date.

    Week 2

    Architecture and wireframes

    Sitemap, URL structure and template definitions. Low-fidelity wireframes for each template so structural arguments happen before visual work.

    Weeks 3–4

    Design system and key templates

    Type scale, colour tokens, component library, then the highest-value templates designed at mobile, tablet and desktop with every state specified.

    Weeks 4–7

    Development

    Front-end build against the performance budget, CMS integration, forms, schema, and analytics configured as part of the work rather than after it. You review on staging continuously, not at the end.

    Week 8

    Quality assurance

    Real-device testing, keyboard and screen-reader passes, Core Web Vitals verification, form and redirect testing, and the written checklist you sign off.

    Week 9

    Launch and monitoring

    Deployment in your low-traffic window with someone monitoring, followed by two weeks of indexation and error watching, then handover documentation.
