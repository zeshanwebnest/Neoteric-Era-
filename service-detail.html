02 — Suitability

Who this is the right answer for.

And, just as usefully, who it is not. Recommending the wrong service costs both of us more than declining the work.

A good fit when

  • Your team needs to publish and edit content without developer help
  • You need a large number of structured pages — services, locations, resources
  • Editorial and blog content is a meaningful part of your acquisition
  • An existing WordPress site has become slow or fragile through plugin sprawl

Probably not the right fit when

  • You need a full transactional storefront with tax and fulfilment handled for you — consider Shopify
  • Your core requirement is complex application logic rather than content — consider Laravel

What is included

  • Custom theme built from your design system, no commercial multipurpose theme
  • Structured content models and reusable block patterns
  • Editor experience tested with the people who will actually use it
  • Performance budget enforced, including plugin weight review
  • Schema markup, clean URLs and Search Console verification
  • Security hardening, backup configuration and update policy

03 — Process

How the work runs.

The five stages below apply across this discipline. Duration varies with scope; the sequence does not.

Stage 1

Scope and success metric

A written brief naming the commercial goal, the constraints, and the single number this work should move. Everything after this is measured against it.

Stage 2

Audit and planning

Technical review of what exists, competitor comparison, and a prioritised plan where each item states its expected effect rather than just its existence.

Stage 3

Build or implement

Executed by our own team against the agreed standards — performance budget, accessibility baseline and measurement configured as part of the work.

Stage 4

Verify

Cross-device and cross-browser testing, keyboard and screen-reader passes, Core Web Vitals verification, and a written checklist you sign off rather than take on trust.

Stage 5

Measure and iterate

Post-launch monitoring against the metric agreed in stage one, then a recommendation on what to do next — which is sometimes nothing.

On timelines Build services typically run six to sixteen weeks depending on scope. Ongoing services (SEO, paid media, conversion work) run as monthly programmes with a six-month minimum for anything competitive. Exact timing is confirmed in the proposal, not estimated here.
