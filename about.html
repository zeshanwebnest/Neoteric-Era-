Neoteric ERA is a digital agency working from Lahore, Pakistan, for clients based predominantly in the United States. We provide website and ecommerce development, search engine optimisation, Google Ads management and digital marketing — as one connected service rather than four products.

The commercial argument for offshore delivery is usually cost. That is the least interesting reason to work with us, and if it is the only reason, the relationship tends not to last. The reason that holds is capability concentration: a team small enough that the person who wrote your templates is the person who set up your conversion tracking, and large enough to cover four disciplines properly.

We are deliberately explicit about where we are. Our working hours overlap the US business day, our written communication is designed for asynchronous review, and every project has a named contact who works on delivery rather than an account manager relaying messages.

A note on this website This site is a working prototype. Statistics, client names, testimonials and project metrics are clearly marked placeholders pending verification against our records. We would rather show you an honest scaffold than invent numbers.

02 — Philosophy

What we believe, stated as decisions.

A philosophy is only meaningful if it rules something out. Here is what each of ours costs us.

Brand philosophy

Clarity outperforms decoration.

Given a choice between an effect that impresses other designers and a layout that a distracted buyer can parse in four seconds, we choose the second. It means our work sometimes looks quieter than a portfolio piece should. We accept that trade.

Mission

Make growth attributable.

Our mission is to give clients a digital operation where the connection between spend and outcome is visible. That means we will refuse work where the client will not allow analytics access — we cannot be accountable for a number we cannot see.

Vision

A partner, not a vendor rotation.

We want to be the team a business keeps through three redesigns and two pivots. That shapes how we quote: we would rather scope a smaller first project honestly than win a large one we will struggle to deliver.

Strategic before tactical

We ask what the business needs to happen before we ask what the page should look like. If the two conflict, the business need wins.

Direct communication

Bad news travels as fast as good news. If a deadline is at risk you hear it the week it becomes at risk, not the week it slips.

Craft in the invisible parts

Semantic markup, keyboard order, redirect maps, alt text. The work nobody compliments is the work that determines whether the visible part performs.

Commercial literacy

We learn your margins, your sales cycle and what a lead is worth. Without that, optimisation advice is guesswork with a chart attached.

Accessibility as baseline

WCAG 2.2 AA is a starting requirement, not an upgrade. It is also, not incidentally, better for search and for conversion.

Iteration over launch theatre

A launch is a hypothesis going live. The interesting work starts the week after, when real behaviour disagrees with our assumptions.

Stage one

Development-only, and hitting a ceiling

The company began as a build shop: WordPress and custom sites delivered to spec. The work was solid and the results were inconsistent, because we had no control over what happened after handover. Sites we were proud of were being marketed badly, and we were being judged on the outcome anyway.

Stage two

Adding search, reluctantly at first

We brought SEO in-house because too many builds were launching into invisibility. The immediate discovery was uncomfortable: several of our own past sites had architecture decisions that made ranking harder. That feedback loop changed how we structure templates permanently.

Stage three

Paid media, for the feedback speed

Google Ads was added less for the revenue than for the learning rate. Paid search tells you within a week which message converts; organic takes a quarter to say the same thing. Running both means the slow channel gets to borrow the fast channel's evidence.

Stage four

Focusing on the US market

We narrowed geographically rather than broadening. Specialising in US search intent, US buying behaviour and US-hours communication produced better work than trying to serve four continents generically.

Now

One team, four disciplines, one report

Today a typical engagement involves people from two or three disciplines from week one, sharing a single set of success metrics. The structural problem we started with — nobody owning the outcome — is the thing the company is now organised to prevent.

How delivery is actually run.

Process documentation is easy to write and easy to ignore. These four are the ones that change what actually ships.

Why only four

  1. 01

    A written brief that predates design

    Nothing is designed until there is a one-page document naming the target metric, the audience, the primary conversion and the constraints. It prevents the most expensive failure mode in agency work: a beautiful deliverable answering the wrong question.

  2. 02

    A performance budget agreed up front

    Target LCP, total JavaScript weight and image budgets are set before the first template is written, then tested at each milestone. Speed treated as a launch-week problem is speed you will not achieve.

  3. 03

    A weekly written update, whether or not you ask

    Three sections: shipped, blocked, needs your decision. Written, not verbal, so there is a record and so you can read it at a time that suits your time zone rather than ours.

  4. 04

    A pre-launch checklist you sign, not trust

    Redirects, tracking, forms, accessibility passes, Core Web Vitals, backups and rollback plan — itemised, tested and handed to you as a document. Launches go wrong in predictable ways; this is the list of them.

06 — Working across time zones

Distance is a logistics problem, and it is solved.

Offshore delivery fails for operational reasons, not technical ones: replies arriving a day late, approvals stalling overnight, launches scheduled at 3am your time. Each of those has a specific fix.

  • Overlapping hours. Our afternoon covers the US East Coast morning. Calls, approvals and urgent changes happen inside a shared window rather than across a gap.
  • Asynchronous by default. Written updates, recorded walkthroughs and annotated staging links mean progress does not depend on catching each other live.
  • Launches in your window. Deployments are scheduled for your low-traffic hours with someone awake and monitoring, not queued and hoped for.
  • US English, US intent. Copy is written in US English and keyword research is done against how American buyers phrase problems, including regional variation.
  • One named contact. Not a rotating support queue. The same person, who works on your project, for its duration.

The team that built your site is the team maintaining it. Nobody has to re-learn your business every time you need a change.
